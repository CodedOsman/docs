# 2.12.1 Water Bottle Safety Briefing And Baseline Launch

## Overview

Rockets are the only propulsion system capable of operating in the vacuum of space — and the physics that launches a water bottle rocket 30 metres into the air is identical to the physics that launches a satellite into orbit. This project builds on the jet engine theory from Project 37, adding a structured experimental investigation of how water fill level, launch angle, fin area, and pressure affect flight performance. Students apply Newton's Third Law in its purest form and experience directly why rocket engineers spend thousands of hours optimising these variables.

## Step-by-Step Assembly

**Lesson 1 – Safety Briefing & Baseline Launch**

|  |  |
| --- | --- |
| **STEP 1** | **Safety Briefing & Zone Setup** |
|  | * Mark a 5 m radius safety circle around the launch pad with chalk or rope * All observers must stand outside this circle before and during every launch * Safety officer role: one student stands beside the launch pad; responsible for confirming area is clear before each launch * Mandatory: safety goggles for the launch operator and all students within 10 m * Maximum pressure: 80 PSI. Count pump strokes for consistency (typically 60–80 strokes) |

|  |  |
| --- | --- |
| **STEP 2** | **Baseline Launch (30% water, 60°, standard fins, 70 PSI)** |
|  | * Fill the bottle to the 30% mark with water; insert rubber stopper firmly * Set launch angle to 60° from horizontal * Pump to 70 PSI (or 65 strokes) * Launch; observe; estimate height by comparison to a known reference (tree, building) * Measure horizontal distance from launch rod to landing point * Record in Row 1 of the data table — this is the baseline for all comparisons |
